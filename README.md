# Conserved developmental networks underlie post-embryonic remodelling in teleosts

Agneesh Barua<sup>1,2,*</sup>, Giulia Campli<sup>1,2</sup>, Marcella Herrera<sup>3</sup>, Saori Miura<sup>3</sup>, Ken Maeda<sup>3</sup>, Yann Gibert<sup>4</sup>, Vincent Laudet<sup>3,5</sup>, Marc Robinson-Rechavi<sup>1,2</sup>

Comparative embryology has revealed a high degree of homology and conservation in early animal development. Beyond the embryo, however, animals exhibit striking variation in post-embryonic developmental strategies. The degree of conservation in genes mediating post-embryonic development, and how they can give rise to phenotypic variation, remains an open question. In this study, we characterised post-embryonic development across five teleost fish families to uncover the links between developmental processes and trait evolution. Comparative gene regulatory networks revealed a conserved set of genes underlying post-embryonic development in teleosts. These genes were organised into discrete modules associated with multiple physiological processes such as aerobic respiration, and synaptic signalling, as well as the formation of major tissue systems, including the eye, musculature, and nervous system. Evolutionary analyses revealed that these genes are generally under purifying selection, but exhibit extensive copy-number turnover across teleosts, with major expansions following the teleost-specific whole-genome duplication and additional lineage-specific gains and losses. Phylogenetic modelling identified significant associations between copy-number variation in these rapidly evolving gene families and phenotypic diversity across teleost lineages in traits such as age of maturity, swimming performance, and body shape. Bulk and single-cell transcriptomic data confirmed expression of these genes in tissues and cell types relevant to the associated phenotypes, and functional perturbation experiments in zebrafish supported their roles in corresponding tissues. Together, these findings indicate that the core genetic toolkit regulating post-embryonic development originated early in teleost evolution and was subsequently refined through gene copy-number dynamics, potentially contributing to the phenotypic diversity across clades.
